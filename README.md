# ASL-number-recognition
Arduino-based wearable glove that recognizes ASL numbers (0–9) using flex and force sensors with audio feedback.
Communication between Deaf or hard of hearing individuals and hearing individuals had often been difficult during everyday interactions because sign language had not been widely understood. A wearable sign language translation glove had been developed to reduce this barrier by detecting hand and finger movements and producing spoken audio in real time. The prototype had used four flex sensors and one force sensor connected to an Arduino Uno microcontroller. Sensor values had been read as analog inputs and had been classified into American Sign Language number gestures from one through ten. After classification, a loudspeaker had played the corresponding audio output. The circuitry had been mounted in an adjustable forearm compartment secured with a Velcro strap to improve comfort and stability. Testing had shown that the system had detected numbers one through ten and had produced the expected audio output after each recognized gesture. Gestures for four and five had been detected most quickly and precisely under the final sensor placement and threshold settings. Future work had included expanding recognition from numbers to the ASL alphabet and common words, adding an inertial measurement unit to capture hand orientation, and reducing circuit size for improved wearability.
